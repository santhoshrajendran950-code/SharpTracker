## Drawbacks found, in order of cost

### 1. The sharps' entries do not beat the close. The picks' CLV comes from the book, not the signal.
Signal CLV (close − sharp's entry) is **−2.02 pts** over 1,391 priced
suggestions, and it gets worse the shorter the favourite: −122..−150 −1.5,
−150..−180 −4.7, −180..−300 −7.0, shorter than −300 −14.7. Only underdogs show
positive CLV (+2.1 pts) and they lose outright (37% hit). Published picks
show **+1.58 pts** because `pickclv.py` measures the *book* price Santhosh
took against the Polymarket close. Backfilled against the sharp's own entry,
the same 60 picks are **+0.03 pts (52% beat)**: on the picks, the signal sits
exactly at the close, and the +1.6 is the book spread — a real, repeatable
edge in **price shopping**, not in the signal. The KPI as defined was
measuring the book spread. **Done 2026-10-07:** both CLVs are now stored on
every pick and printed everywhere; the KPI is the signal CLV.

### 2. The overrides turned a 31-15 rule into a 32-33 record.
Published picks split: default window **20-14 (+28%)**, override **12-19
(−26% [−54, +1])**, pre-v4/dog picks 0-?. The pick'em band across *all* v4
suggestions (not just the overridden trader) is 58-66, −12.1% [−26, +2] on 86
games — now above the 40-game bar set in EXP-020. The three override entries
were set from the same 20–32 signals that seeded `trusted.json`; the live
data contradicts all of the dog-ward widening. The favourite-ward widening
(justaluckydude, CandleHammerDrums) is 15-4 in-window but carries −8 pts CLV
each: they win while the market moves against them, which at 19 picks is
luck until proven otherwise.
**Improvement:** retire the `price_min` override (pick'em) now — the pre-registered
threshold is met. Keep `price_max` at ½u until 40 games.

### 3. Sizing lost 19 units more than flat.
Published picks were +2.4% flat and −17% sized. The 90+ score band (hold 5%+
and wager ≥ $25K) is 19-26 live and received the largest units; the 70–79
band is 8-2 and received the smallest. On all 842 v4 suggestions the 80–89
band is the worst (−19.3% [−38, −1]) and 90+ second worst; <65 is break-even.
The research rebuild of the same month: s5 −27.3% vs flat −22.0%. Five sizing
versions, seven OOS football months, and now the live window all say the same
thing. **Improvement:** flat 1u on every graded pick (EXP-022 strengthened).
If a size step must exist, it should not come from the score.

### 4. The score's inputs are not ordering outcomes.
- **Stake $10–25K is the worst stake band**: −17.2% [−29, −6], n=254. The v4
  table gives it 35 points, nearly the $25K tier. Under $10K is break-even.
- **Fresh loses, stale wins**: fresh −6.9% [−12, −1] n=945; stale +4.5%
  [−4, +13] n=456. v4 gives fresh +10 and s5 takes a unit off stale.
  "Stale" means the market has already moved since the sharp entered — that
  is the one trait with the right sign here, and the rules penalise it.
- **Hold bands are flat** (2–3% −1.9, 3–5% −2.0, 5%+ −2.4). The 45-point
  hold component separates nothing in this window.
- **Consensus 2–4 are all negative**; single-sharp rows are the best bucket
  (+2.8%). Already a prior; now a fourth sample.
**Improvement:** EXP-026 — refit nothing; instead drop the fresh bonus and the
stale penalty, and move the $10–25K wager tier down to the $5K points. Test
on the 99 research windows first (`hist_cuts.py` can take a modified
`lib/check.py` points table).

### 5. The board floods and contradicts itself.
1,401 suggestions on 466 games is 3 per game on average; **NFL averages 11
per game**, one game had 38. **18% of markets had both sides suggested**, and
39% of all suggestions sat on a market where the board also showed the other
side. One trader (primm) is 16% of everything shown, at 50% hit and +0.4%.
Two-thirds of suggestions were first flagged within an hour of kickoff.
**Improvement:** when both sides of a market qualify, show neither as a play
(mark the market "contested") — the same logic already applied to one
wallet's two-sided book. Cap any one trader at a share of the board. Surface
the hours-to-kickoff so a reader knows what is actionable.

### 6. The trend is down.
By week: +26% (Sep 7, n=29), −0.5%, +0.3%, **−8.8% [−17, −0]** (Sep 28),
**−18.8% [−32, −0]** (Oct 5, n=54). The two most recent weeks are the only
ones with intervals excluding zero, and both are negative. The published
picks' sized line: +19% → −18% → 0-2. Whatever worked on Sep 19–27 (the
replayed window the rules were tuned on) has not worked since.

### 7. Market types: totals lose live, win in research.
Live totals 141-157, −9.9% [−20, +0] on suggestions and 5-11 on picks;
research totals are the best market type in NBA and MLB. Unresolved;
EXP-019 stands. Spreads −4.6%, moneylines +0.4% with the worst CLV.

### 8. Sports: tennis and NHL are the only positive leagues, and they are the ones with no graded picks.
ATP 76-44 +12.2% [−1, +27] n=120; NHL 16-6 +33% [+6, +58] n=22. Both have
awful signal CLV (ATP −5.8) — the sharps are moving these thin markets
themselves, so a follower cannot get their price. MLB −13.4% [−24, −2] n=219
is the one league with an interval excluding zero, and it is negative; MLB
picks were 3-9.

## What held up
- The **−122..−150** band is the best price band on every surface:
  suggestions 81-52 (+4.1%), published picks 10-3 with 13/13 beating the close.
- **Single-sharp** favourites inside the window: 19-8, +16.6% [−8, +40], and
  the only pick-rule cut with positive signal CLV.
- The nightly settlement, CLV capture and the ledgers worked every day; this
  evaluation exists because of them.
